# Glass Control Room

Glass Control Room treats software operations as a field of visible forces rather than a stack of hidden configuration files. It is one native macOS window: the window material provides the only translucent layer, while the work itself remains crisp, solid, and legible on standard macOS surfaces.

Color is diagnostic, never atmospheric. The real desktop supplies the environment; the app adds no gradient, colored fog, or branded wash. Green confirms continuity, amber asks for attention, and system blue identifies the primary action. Everything else stays monochrome so state can be read before labels are parsed.

Scale follows the rhythm of a control room. Large quiet headings establish location, compact monospaced readings provide evidence, and circular status nodes create a measured pulse across the composition. The recurring conduit is the movement's signature form: separate systems are shown as one continuous path, refined through countless adjustments until flow, interruption, and completion are immediately visible.

Composition favors one stable horizon with shallow layers rather than a collage of cards. The native traffic lights belong to this one window, not to an inset imitation window. Use system buttons, source-list selection, separators, and grouped surfaces for hierarchy; reserve translucency for the surrounding window material.

Text is sparse, active, and exact. Labels name what a person can control; status copy says what happened and what to do next. Typography is integrated as instrumentation, with a humane grotesque for decisions and a precise mono for paths, revisions, commands, and receipts. Every alignment, radius, and optical contrast must demonstrate deep expertise and the kind of polish produced only by repeated, painstaking refinement.
